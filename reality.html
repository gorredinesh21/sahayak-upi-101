01 · Who fixes what today

Three tiers already exist — only one of them is human

Before asking "can an agent do this?", know who does it today. Money-return mechanics are already automated at the bottom. Humans live in the middle and top — in different jobs than you'd guess.

~80–85% of stuck cases
Tier 1 · The Rails — NPCI + bank core systems

⚡ Fully automatic, no human ever touches it

  • Auto-reversal on explicit failure: debit done + credit failed (Z5/Z8) → reversal fires by itself, usually lands in minutes–hours, guideline deadline T+1 (worst case ~3 days)
  • Pending auto-expiry: a hanging txn (T1/Z6) is deemed failed & released automatically, typically 30 min–24 h
  • Orphan-debit cleanup: most duplicate debits self-reverse the same way
  • Settlement reconciliation: end-of-day matching between banks, machine-to-machine
▼ when the rails' answer isn't enough, it lands on… ▼
the ticket factory
Tier 2 · The Ops Floor — Paytm 24×7 chat/call + bank ops teams

🧑‍💻 Humans… mostly reading a screen out loud

  • Reassurance tickets: "your money is safe, reversal initiated, expect it by 14:02 tomorrow" — said a few thousand times a day, info already visible on their dashboard
  • Complaint filing: past-SLA cases → human raises the ticket with RRN into the bank/NPCI portal and tracks it
  • SLA chasing: ops dashboards + manual follow-ups when a reversal stalls or a complaint sits
  • Translation & channel handling: saying the same thing in Hindi/Telugu/English across chat, call, WhatsApp

This is the volume layer — the bulk of headcount and cost (₹80–150 per ticket), and it's exactly the layer that's information work, not judgment work.

▼ and the cases machines can't decide land on… ▼
~15%, all the pain
Tier 3 · The Judgment Desk — risk, disputes, legal

⚖️ Humans deciding, not just informing

  • Wrong-account transfers: a *successful* payment to the wrong VPA — no rail reverses it; needs beneficiary-bank consent or legal muscle
  • Fraud response: scam detected → freezing via the cybercrime helpline (1930) / portal, banks coordinating manually — speed decides if money is catchable
  • Merchant disputes: paid but goods never came → chargeback-style arbitration
  • Executive escalations: ombudsman defense, viral complaints, VIP cases

The rails solve none of these automatically. This layer is humans because someone must hold authority, not just information.

📉 Failure taxonomy — what happens to 10,000 payment attempts

NPCI splits failures into Business Declines (BD) — the user's fault: low balance, wrong PIN, limit, bad VPA — and Technical Declines (TD) — the system's fault. Attempt-level success runs ~85–95%. So of 10,000 attempts:

✓ ~9,000 succeed
✅ ~9,000 succeed (90%)
🟠 ~950 business declines (8–12% of attempts) — money never left
🔴 ~30–80 technical declines (0.3–0.8%) — the system's failures
Inside business declines (no official % split — order per industry analyses): 💸 insufficient funds — the largest single reason🔑 wrong UPI PIN📊 daily/monthly limit🪪 invalid VPA / account
🔴 Zoom: inside those ~50 technical declines — our 4 scenes

⚠️ Nobody publishes this split — not NPCI, not banks. What follows is our directional model built from the code families (clean upfront declines leave no debit; timeout/reversal codes are the rarer, uglier paths) — treat as design estimates, not data.

🧮 The scale math that lands with judges (directional)

At 24.5 B transactions/month, even a 0.3–0.8% technical decline rate = ~75–195 million technically-failed payments every month. Of those, our model says ~25–35% involve actual debited money (pending + reversal + duplicate) = ~20–65 million stuck-money situations monthly. If even 5% of those need a human today: 1–3.5 million support tickets/month at ₹80–150 each ≈ ₹10–50 crore/month of support cost — before counting the trust damage and the Twitter complaints. Rare per-transaction. Enormous per-month.

👻 The invisible category — Scene 4 exists in no failure statistic

Wrong-account transfers and UPI fraud never appear in TD/BD numbers — because the payment technically SUCCEEDED. They surface only in complaints and crime data: RBI Ombudsman logged 13.34 lakh total complaints in FY25 (+13.6%), with mobile/electronic banking at 16.86% (and note: back in FY22, digital payments were the #1 category at 42% — the volume migrated to in-app support, which is exactly where Sahayak sits). Every "successful" ₹19,000 sent to a scammer is a zero in the failure dashboard. The worst day in payments is invisible in payments statistics — that's why the agent needs relationship signals, not just error codes.

07 · The Strategies

Three truths. Three battle plans.

The 30-second answer names the problems; this is how Sahayak kills each one. Every strategy follows the same anatomy: trigger → agent moves → sponsor engines → demo moment → the number that proves it.

kills Truth 1 · nobody tells the customer

📣 Strategy 1 — "Say It First"

The Proactive Narration Engine. Win the race against the customer's panic: the first message about a stuck payment should come from us — within 60 seconds — not from a support queue after 3 hours of fear.

⚡ payment event webhook fires→ 🧠 classify failure state + real ETA (from case memory)→ 🌐 compose in customer's language→ 📲 WhatsApp / SMS / voice note→ 🔁 update at every state change→ ✅ close with confirmation receipt

🧩 How it works

Today the info exists on a dashboard but a human must be asked to read it aloud. We invert the flow: the event pushes the story out. ETA isn't generic — case memory knows this bank + this code historically resolves in ~26h, so we say that. Guardrail: one thread per case, max 4 messages, unsubscribe word honored — proactive ≠ spammy.

🎬 Demo moment (Scene 1)

Payment fails live → within seconds the phone on stage buzzes with a Hindi voice note: "दिनेश जी, आपके ₹2,800 वापस आ रहे हैं, कल 14:02 तक — रसीद नंबर 4102…155।" The customer never opens support. That's the ticket that was never born.

n8n — webhook listener on payment events; the comms workflow; delivery retries; the execution log = proof it ran Sarvam — Hindi/regional voice notes (bulbul TTS) + STT for voice replies; understanding of the reply itself is handled by Gemini Cognee — case memory: per-bank, per-code historical resolution times → the real ETA in the first message
🎯 Target: time-to-first-information hours → <60 seconds · reassurance tickets deflected ~entirely (the ₹80–150 × thousands/day layer)
kills Truth 2 · stalled reversals never self-heal

⏱️ Strategy 2 — "The Machine That Never Forgets"

Stall-hunter + auto-filer. The rails give up silently; humans forget. Software forgets nothing. Every case gets a timer armed at birth, and the T+1 tick either confirms money landed — or fires the whole filing-and-chasing sequence by itself.

⏰ T+1 timer fires (case open since yesterday)→ 🔎 re-verify: did reversal land?→ 🧾 no? → classify the stall → file complaint with RRN itself→ 🔁 arm T+3 chase → escalate ladder→ 📣 narrate every step to customer

🧩 How it works

A deterministic state machine per case: open → verified → filed → chased → escalated → closed. No state can be skipped and no case can be dropped — that's the whole point. Filing is idempotent (one complaint per RRN, ever). The 30-day rung (ombudsman) is tracked too, so the ladder unlocks on schedule, not on anger.

🎬 Demo moment (Scene 2)

31-hour-pending payment on the dashboard. Agent already filed CMP-338821 (judges watch the filing happen in the thinking timeline), T+1 chase is armed, and a live timer ticks toward the next bank ping. The ops-human this replaces isn't in the room — that's the point.

n8n — the backbone: scheduled SLA timers, retry-with-backoff on bank queries, complaint-desk API calls, escalation fan-out. Its execution log IS the audit trail — show it live to judges Cognee — learns per-bank stall behaviour ("this bank's Z5 stalls resolve on the 2nd nudge at T+2"), so chase timing adapts instead of blasting on a fixed schedule Sarvam — every state change becomes a customer update in their language; voice for low-literacy users
🎯 Target: zombie complaints 5–7 days → <24h median · forgotten cases → zero by construction
kills Truth 3 · wrong account & fraud have no auto path

🚨 Strategy 3 — "Minutes, Not Days"

The evidence flashlight. We can't (and won't) reverse a successful payment — but recoverability is a race against money hopping between mule accounts. What wins the race: detect in seconds, assemble the complete dossier instantly, hand humans and the victim a ready-to-fire action kit.

🚩 signature detected (first-time VPA + amount ≫ usual + reputation flags)→ 🧠 hold: no auto-action — assemble full case file→ 📤 one alert to the risk desk + victim guidance kit (1930 / cybercrime portal, step-by-step)→ 🎙️ Sarvam voice walks the panicked customer through freezing steps live→ ⚖️ human decides — dossier already complete

🧩 How it works

Speed × completeness. Today the victim's own alertness is the detection system and the evidence gathering starts days later, scattered across calls. Sahayak detects at payment time from signals already in hand, builds the dossier (timeline, RRN, VPA reputation, amount anomaly, device) in ~40 seconds, and — critically — calms and directs the victim in real time, because a panicked victim who acts in minute 2 freezes money; one who acts on day 3 doesn't.

🎬 Demo moment (Scene 4)

₹19,000 to a first-time, fraud-flagged VPA. The agent visibly refuses to auto-act — instead: dossier assembled, risk-desk alerted with one complete case file, and a steady Hindi voice note guiding the customer through the 1930 freeze steps. The restraint is the intelligence.

Cognee — the fraud-pattern memory: VPAs linked to prior cases, mule-account clustering, recurring scam signatures — detection sharpens with every case (the LEARN loop) n8n — parallel fan-out: risk-desk alert + customer comms + case record fire simultaneously, not sequentially; every escalation path is a visible workflow Sarvam — the emotional-heavy-lifter: a calm scripted voice guiding a scared victim beat-by-beat through the freeze process in their language
🎯 Target: time-to-human-with-complete-evidence days → minutes · evidence completeness 100% · freeze-success window actually winnable

09 · The 30-Second Answer

When a judge asks the question — say this

🎤 Memorize this
"You're right — for most stuck payments, the rail itself returns the money automatically, usually within hours. But three things are still true. One: nobody tells the customer that — the single biggest support volume is humans narrating a waiting screen, at ₹80–150 a ticket. Two: when a reversal stalls past its deadline, the rails never self-heal — today a human has to notice, file with the RRN, and chase. Three: the scary cases — wrong account, fraud — have no automatic path at all, and every minute of delay burns recoverable money. Sahayak takes tier two entirely — the informing, filing, and chasing — and makes tier three minutes instead of days by handing humans a complete case file. It never touches the money itself.
